    Welcome to Fishamongus!

    Your fishing log: what you caught, where, on what, and in what conditions.

    • Log a catch in a few taps. Pick the fish and the lure, add a photo if you like. Location, weather and tides fill in by themselves.
    • Tap Start fishing when your line goes in. The app times the session and tracks your lure, so you learn which lures catch the most fish per hour.
    • See what works. Stats show which lures catch fish in which weather and tides, and Trips plans your next outing.
    • Your log stays on this phone. Nothing is uploaded or shared. Back it up from Settings now and then.
    • Location is used to record where each fish was caught and to look up the weather and tides there. Your phone asks when you tap Get started.
    • Camera and photos are used only when you add a photo to a catch.

      Settings

      Night mode turns the screen dim red, so it doesn't spoil your night vision at dawn or after dark.

      While a session runs and Fishamongus! is open, your path is saved with the session and shown on the map. Phones pause web apps in the background, so keep the app on screen while you move. Routes stay on this device.

      Tap 🎤 Say it on the Now screen and say what you caught – for example “two Dungeness, kept, crab pot” or “largemouth on a Senko, released”. On Android, press and hold the app icon for Say a catch, Log a catch and Start fishing.

      Your log is saved only on this phone. A backup file you keep somewhere else (iCloud Drive, Google Drive, your computer) is what saves it if the phone is lost or the app is deleted.

      Import a log

      Bring in catches you logged somewhere else: a spreadsheet (CSV) from another fishing app or one you kept yourself, or a Fishamongus! backup. GPX, KML and KMZ files from GPS units, fish finders, Google Earth or other apps bring in their places as spots. You check how the columns match and see a preview first; catches already in your log are skipped. On a computer you can also drop the file onto the window.

      Add someone else's log

      Add a fishing partner's backup, or your old phone's, to this log. Nothing of yours is replaced: catches already here are skipped, and their spots join yours when they're within 100 m.

      How do I install the app?

      iPhone: open the app's link in Safari, tap Share (the square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen. Android: open the link in Chrome, then tap Install (or ⋮ → Install app). Installed, it opens full screen and works with no signal.

      Where is my log kept?

      Only on this phone, in the app's own storage. Nothing is uploaded. On iPhone, deleting the app from the home screen deletes its data too, so export a backup first. A backup file includes photos, spots and trips, and Restore loads it on any phone.

      How do I log shellfish?

      Tap Shellfish above the species list, and pick Saltwater or Freshwater to shorten the list. Choose the gear (pot, ring net, clam gun…) and, for crab, the sex, shell and width. For Washington limits, open Limits and tap Add Puget Sound crab limits or Add Washington clam & oyster limits. Before eating clams, oysters or mussels, check the Department of Health Shellfish Safety Map.

      Where does the shellfish beach status come from?

      From the Washington Department of Health's Shellfish Safety Map data, updated hourly, for public beaches near saltwater places. It's a helper, not the final word: biotoxin closures can start without warning, so check the Shellfish Safety Map or call the shellfish hotline (1-800-562-5632) before you harvest.

      Can I log a catch without touching the screen much?

      Yes. On the Now screen tap 🎤 Say it and say the fish, the lure or gear, how many and whether you kept them. The catch is saved with GPS, weather and tide, and Undo takes it back if it heard you wrong. Settings → Hands-free logging also gives you a one-tap link for each of your usual catches: on Android, put it on the home screen or in a Google Assistant routine. On iPhone, links opened from the Shortcuts app open in Safari, which keeps a separate log from the home-screen app, so use the 🎤 button in the app instead.

      What are the catch record card reminders?

      In Washington, kept salmon, steelhead, sturgeon, halibut and Puget Sound Dungeness crab go on a catch record card. Stats counts what you've kept for each card, and in the month before a card is due (fish cards April 30, summer crab cards October 1, winter crab cards February 1) the Now screen reminds you to report it. The app's counts are a check only – record each fish in ink or in the eCRC as you keep it.

      Where does the river flow come from?

      From the nearest USGS stream gauge within about 6 miles, for spots and places that aren't tidal. It shows the flow in cubic feet per second and whether it's rising or falling over the last 3 hours. Catches made near a gauge save the flow, and Stats adds a River flow condition once three catches have one.

      What are "Best days ahead" and the weather alerts?

      Best days ahead on the Now screen rates the next two weeks where you are: bite times (solunar), the forecast (wind, storms, heavy rain), tides by the coast and, once you have five or more catches nearby, how close each day's conditions are to the ones you catch fish in. Tap a day to plan a trip for its best hours. Weather alerts are the National Weather Service's active warnings and advisories (US only) for where you are, your spots and upcoming trips, including marine ones like small craft advisories.

      Can I see boat launches, depths and my route on the map?

      Yes. On the map, tap the layers button. Boat launches & access shows WDFW's boat ramps, hand launches, fishing piers and shore-fishing sites in Washington; tap one for directions or to save it as a spot. NOAA nautical charts adds depths, channels and buoys on the water (not for navigation). My session routes shows where you went: while a session runs and the app is open, your route is recorded (turn it off in Settings). Export spots and routes (GPX) in Settings saves them for a GPS or fish finder, and Import a log brings in GPX, KML or KMZ places as spots.

      Can I find a place by name instead of using GPS?

      Yes. In a catch, a spot or a trip, type in the Find a place box under the location – a lake, river, boat launch, beach or town, then tap Search. Your saved spots come first; other places come from OpenStreetMap, nearest first. On the map, tap the magnifying glass. You can also paste coordinates (47.144, -122.675 or 47°08′38″N 122°40′30″W) or a Google Maps link. Searching other places needs a connection; your spots work offline.

      Can I bring in my log from another app or a spreadsheet?

      Yes. Export your catches from the other app as a spreadsheet (CSV) – or save your own spreadsheet as CSV – then use Import a log in Settings (on a computer you can also drop the file onto the window). The app matches the columns by name: date and time, fish, how many, lure, length, weight, released or kept, latitude and longitude, spot and notes. Check the matches and the preview, then import. Catches already in your log are skipped, weather is filled in for catches with a GPS position, and Undo removes the whole import.

      Can I add my fishing partner's catches?

      Yes. Ask them to make a backup (Settings → Backup), send you the file, then use Add someone else's log in Settings. Their catches are added with their name, their spots join yours when they're within 100 m, and anything already in your log is skipped. Moving to a new phone works the same way.

      Why is there no tide on a catch?

      Tides come from NOAA and cover US coasts. They're recorded within about 15 mi of a NOAA tide station, or at spots set to Saltwater. Spots set to Freshwater never show tides. With no signal, the tide is added later when you're online.

      What does Start fishing do?

      It times your session and keeps track of the lure on your line (tap it to change lures). Catches you log are added to the session. Stats then shows fish per hour for each lure, which is fairer than raw counts. A session left running over 12 hours ends 30 minutes after your last catch.

      How are the best lures and best hours chosen?

      From your own catches: the app counts fish caught in each condition (sky, pressure, temperature, wind, time of day, tide, moon). Counts favour lures you use most; fishing sessions and planned trips record hours fished, which gives fish per hour.

      Where does the estimated weight come from?

      For 12 common freshwater species (black bass, bluegill, crappie, walleye, perch, channel catfish, rainbow trout, wiper and carp), fisheries "standard weight" equations give the weight of a typical fish of that length. Your fish may be heavier or lighter, so it's saved as an estimate and doesn't count as a personal best.

      How do bag limits work?

      Set a daily limit for any fish in Settings → Daily bag limits. Fish marked Kept today count towards it. The app warns you before you save one over the limit, but it doesn't know your local rules – always check them.

      What are bite times?

      Solunar theory says fish feed more when the moon is overhead or underfoot (major periods, about 2 hours) and around moonrise and moonset (minor periods, about 1 hour), and most on days near the new and full moon. The app works these out from the moon's position where you are. It's a popular idea rather than proven science, so Stats → Solunar shows whether it holds for your own catches.

      How does automatic backup work?

      Choose how often in Settings → Backup. On a computer's Chrome (and some Android phones) you can pick a backup file once, and the app keeps it up to date by itself. On iPhone, web apps can't save to iCloud on their own, so the app reminds you when a backup is due and one tap opens the share sheet – choose Save to Files, then iCloud Drive. To restore, use Restore from backup on any phone.

      How do I log a fish I caught earlier?

      Add the photo you took: the app offers to use the time and place stored in it. Or change "Caught at" and the weather and tide are looked up for that time.

      Does the map work with no signal?

      Map areas you've already looked at while online are kept on the phone and show without signal. Open the map at your spots once at home to keep them handy.
